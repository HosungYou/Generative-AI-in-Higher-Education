# Risk of Bias Assessment

## GenAI Effectiveness in Higher Education Meta-Analysis

**Assessment Framework**: Adapted Cochrane Risk of Bias Tool
**Assessment Date**: 2026-01-24
**Assessors**: H.Y. (Primary), R.A. (Secondary)

---

## Assessment Methodology

Given the heterogeneity of study designs (RCTs, quasi-experimental, and other controlled designs), we employed a hybrid risk of bias assessment approach:

- **RCTs (n = 34)**: Assessed using Cochrane RoB 2.0 domains
- **Quasi-experimental (n = 18)**: Assessed using ROBINS-I principles
- **Other controlled designs (n = 13)**: Assessed using adapted criteria

### Domain Definitions

| Domain | RCTs | Quasi-experimental |
|:-------|:-----|:-------------------|
| D1: Randomization/Selection | Randomization process | Selection of participants |
| D2: Deviations from interventions | Deviations from intended interventions | Confounding |
| D3: Missing outcome data | Missing outcome data | Missing data |
| D4: Outcome measurement | Measurement of outcome | Measurement of outcomes |
| D5: Selective reporting | Selection of reported result | Selection of reported result |
| D6: Overall | Overall bias judgment | Overall bias judgment |

### Rating Scale

- **Low Risk** (L): The study is judged to be at low risk of bias
- **Some Concerns** (S): The study is judged to raise some concerns
- **High Risk** (H): The study is judged to be at high risk of bias
- **Not Applicable** (NA): Domain not applicable to study design

## Sensitivity Analysis: Impact of Risk of Bias

### Meta-Regression with RoB as Moderator

To assess whether RoB influences effect estimates, we conducted meta-regression with overall RoB judgment as a categorical moderator.

| RoB Level | k | g | 95% CI | p |
|:----------|:-:|:-:|:------:|:-:|
| Low Risk | 38 | 0.587 | [0.341, 0.833] | < .001 |
| Some Concerns | 24 | 0.672 | [0.378, 0.966] | < .001 |
| High Risk | 3 | 0.748 | [−0.124, 1.620] | .092 |

**Omnibus test for moderation**: Qₘ(2) = 0.89, p = .641

**Interpretation**: No significant difference in effect sizes across RoB levels, suggesting that overall findings are not substantially biased by study quality. However, the slightly larger effects in higher-risk studies warrant continued vigilance.

### Excluding High-Risk Studies

When excluding the 3 high-risk studies:
- Pooled effect: g = 0.612, 95% CI [0.386, 0.838]
- Change from full sample: Δg = −0.010 (1.6% reduction)
- Conclusion: Results are robust to exclusion of high-risk studies

## Domain-Specific Concerns and Mitigations

### D1: Randomization/Selection Bias

**Primary Concerns:**
- 52% of studies were RCTs with adequate randomization
- Quasi-experimental studies used intact classroom assignments
- Some studies had unclear allocation concealment

**Mitigation in Synthesis:**
- Sensitivity analysis restricting to RCTs only (g = 0.587)
- Results remain significant and similar in magnitude

### D2: Deviations from Intended Interventions / Confounding

**Primary Concerns:**
- Difficulty blinding participants to AI vs. non-AI conditions
- Potential for differential co-interventions
- Quasi-experimental designs vulnerable to confounding

**Mitigation in Synthesis:**
- Most deviations were balanced across groups
- Meta-regression showed no significant moderation by design

### D3: Missing Outcome Data

**Primary Concerns:**
- Attrition rates generally low (mean = 8.3%)
- Most studies used intention-to-treat or reported complete case analyses

**Assessment:**
- Low concern; minimal impact expected on effect estimates

### D4: Outcome Measurement

**Primary Concerns:**
- Metacognitive outcomes predominantly self-report (64%)
- Potential for social desirability bias
- Some cognitive outcomes used unvalidated researcher-developed tests

**Mitigation in Synthesis:**
- GRADE assessment incorporated measurement concerns
- Moderator analysis by measurement type planned for future work

### D5: Selective Reporting

**Assessment:**
- All studies reported pre-specified primary outcomes
- Some studies had registered protocols available
- No evidence of selective outcome reporting detected

## Recommendations for Primary Research

Based on this RoB assessment, we recommend that future GenAI education studies:

1. **Use randomized designs** when ethically and practically feasible
2. **Pre-register protocols** on platforms like OSF or AsPredicted
3. **Report allocation concealment** methods explicitly
4. **Use validated outcome measures** with established psychometric properties
5. **Include behavioral/trace measures** for metacognitive outcomes to complement self-report
6. **Report attrition** by condition with reasons
7. **Conduct intention-to-treat** analyses as primary
