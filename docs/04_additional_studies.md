## Part C: Study Details for High-Priority Candidates

### 1. Urban et al. (2024) - Creative Problem-Solving

```
Title: ChatGPT Improves Creative Problem-Solving Performance in
       University Students: An Experimental Study
Journal: Computers & Education, Vol. 215, Article 105031
Sample: N = 145 (77 ChatGPT vs. 68 control)
Design: RCT
Effect Sizes:
  - Self-efficacy: d = 0.65
  - Quality: d = 0.69
  - Elaboration: d = 0.61
  - Originality: d = 0.55
Institution: Czech Academy of Sciences, Charles University
```

### 2. Essel et al. (2024) - Cognitive Skills

```
Title: ChatGPT effects on cognitive skills of undergraduate students
Journal: Computers and Education: Artificial Intelligence, Vol. 6
Sample: N = 125 (60 experiment, 65 control)
Design: Mixed-methods RCT
Context: Research Methodology course, flipped classroom
Location: Ghana
Key Outcomes: Critical thinking, creative thinking, reflective thinking
```

### 3. Gan et al. (2024) - Medical Education

```
Title: Integrating ChatGPT in Orthopedic Education for Medical
       Undergraduates: Randomized Controlled Trial
Journal: Journal of Medical Internet Research, Vol. 26
Sample: N = 129 medical students
Design: RCT
ChatGPT Accuracy: 70.60% on orthopedics MCQs
Trial Registration: Chinese Clinical Trial Registry Chictr2300071774
```

### 4. Geng & Razali (2025) - Creativity RCT

```
Title: Can ChatGPT enhance business student creativity? Evidence from
       a randomised controlled trial
Journal: Studies in Higher Education
Sample: N = 1,190 undergraduates
Disciplines: Marketing, Management, Entrepreneurship
Design: RCT
Key Finding: Effect varies by discipline
  - Marketing/Management: Enhanced creativity
  - Entrepreneurship: Little to no effect
```

### 5. Knowledge Retention Study (2025) - Cognitive Dependency Evidence

```
Title: ChatGPT as a cognitive crutch: Evidence from a randomized
       controlled trial on knowledge retention
Journal: Computers & Education: Artificial Intelligence
Sample: N = 120 undergraduates
Design: RCT
Key Finding: ChatGPT users scored significantly lower on retention
  - ChatGPT group: 57.5%
  - Control group: 68.5%
  - Cohen's d = 0.68 (medium-large effect)
```

**This study directly supports the Cognitive Dependency Hypothesis!**
